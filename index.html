The Opportunity

The 2020 Credit Jump Start Seminar was a single event with a strong demand signal. Atlanta's Black middle class is actively seeking financial tools, not financial lectures, there's a meaningful difference, and most financial events get it wrong.

Majic 107.5's audience, Black professionals and families, 35–54, has the income, the ambition, and the skepticism. They've been talked down to by financial institutions their entire lives. An event that speaks to them as intelligent adults will fill a room.

Strategic Frame

A summit, not a seminar. Summit implies peer exchange and practical takeaways. Seminar implies sitting while someone explains things you should already know. The language choice signals respect before they walk in the door.

Three tracks: credit building, wealth creation, and business funding. Attendees self-select. Every session ends with an actionable next step, not a sales pitch.

Event Architecture

Score repair, disputing errors, building credit from scratch. Hands-on workshop format. Real scenarios, not hypotheticals. Take-home action plan.
Track B, Wealth Architecture
Investing basics, real estate entry points, retirement for the self-employed. Panel + Q&A with Atlanta-specific market context.
Track C, Business Funding
Small business loans, grants, pitch prep, banking relationships. Lender and CDFI representation on panel.

The 1-on-1 Clinic
Pre-scheduled 15-minute private sessions with financial counselors. Most valuable component. Limited availability creates registration urgency.

Session Provider Model, Who Shows Up and Why

Session providers are not paid to attend, and they don't need to be. These are licensed credit counselors, CFPs, mortgage brokers, CDFI loan officers, SBA representatives, and fee-only financial advisors who are currently paying $150–$500 per qualified lead through digital advertising. A pre-booked 45-minute private session with a financially motivated Black professional who just sat through a keynote about credit and wealth, and then specifically chose to book time, is one of the highest-quality leads available anywhere in Atlanta.

The pitch to providers: "You'll spend a Saturday delivering 4–6 pre-qualified consultations with people who already trust the station that invited them. What does your typical client acquisition cost?" They do the math. They show up.

Revenue split: 75% to provider / 25% to Urban One. Providers earn meaningful compensation for their time while Urban One captures session revenue on top of sponsorships. The split also gives providers skin in the game, they'll promote the event to their own networks and encourage session bookings, extending Urban One's reach at zero additional marketing cost.

Keynote speakers are different. Rashad Bilal, Patrice Washington, and Ryan Glover are headlining a room, that's a paid speaking engagement covered by the Keynote Sponsor at $7,500–$10,000. Session providers and keynote speakers are two entirely separate asks with two entirely separate models.

The Gap in the Market

Every major city has a late-night institution. New York has The Tonight Show. Los Angeles has everything. Atlanta, the third-largest media market in the Southeast, home to Tyler Perry, a thriving film industry, and more celebrity residents per capita than almost anywhere, has nothing like this. No live talk show experience. No ticketed, produced evening where an Atlanta host interviews Atlanta and national names in front of a live crowd.

Ryan Cameron is the only person in Atlanta radio who has the credibility, the relationships, the on-air presence, and 30 years of audience trust to anchor this. Urban One's network gives him access to guests that a cold production company could never secure. That combination is the moat.

Why This Has Never Happened

Ryan has the audience. He has the relationships. He has the format instincts built over three decades of live radio. What he's never had is a production framework that translates those instincts into a ticketed live experience people pay to attend. This concept gives him that, and gives Urban One a property that is genuinely unprecedented in this market.

This is the pilot. One night. Fully produced. If it works, and it will, it becomes quarterly. Then monthly. Then a property with a streaming conversation, a sponsor portfolio, and a name that travels.

Show Format, Two Hours, Live
The Monologue
Ryan opens solo. 8–10 minutes. Atlanta news, culture, pop culture, the room. Sets the tone immediately. This is where 30 years of voice pays off in the first 10 minutes.
Guest Segment 1, The Legend
Atlanta icon or cultural figure. Long-form conversation, 15–20 minutes. The kind of interview that doesn't happen in a 4-minute morning show window. Depth is the differentiator.
Guest Segment 2, The Draw
National name with Atlanta connection, film, music, sports, business. The booking that sells tickets beyond Ryan's core audience and gets the event press coverage.
The Musical Act
Atlanta artist, emerging or established. 2–3 songs. Live band backing. Not a DJ set, a performance. This is a show, not a party.
Guest Segment 3, The Unexpected

Health systems, insurance brands, credit unions, and food and beverage companies have been trying to reach this audience for years through gospel music. The audience is there. What has been missing is an event architecture that gives sponsors something to actually activate. Revival Edition is that architecture.
